The brief, in four parts

1. What is visible

Shipments by distributor, monthly, for 24 months, and they are clean. The owner can see who buys, how much, and in which months. The top 12 distributors account for 54% of channel revenue, and the ranking has barely moved in two years.

2. What is missing

Profit per distributor for 101 of the 132. Sell-through at retail, which is collected by nobody. Inventory ageing at distributor level. Without those three, every channel decision is being made on the one number that flatters the channel most: shipments in.

3. Where spend and sales line up

They mostly do not. Incentive money follows shipments, so it concentrates on the largest distributors whether or not their retailers sell the product through. An estimated $610K of last year's $1.9M incentive spend went to distributors whose sell-through fell year over year. The program is paying for loading, not for selling.

4. Three fixes, ranked

The three changes most likely to pay back first, in order. Each one names what I found, what it costs, and the fix.

Fix 1 · Pays back first

Pay for sell-through, not just sell-in

What I found: incentives are earned on shipments into the distributor, with no second condition. Distributors can earn in full while their retailers' sales fall.

What it costs: roughly $610K a year paid against declining sell-through, on the sample year's figures, plus the margin given up in the discount itself.

The fix: an incentive redesign with two triggers. A base earning on sell-in, and the larger share earned only when sell-through grows. Pilot it with 20 distributors for one quarter before rolling it out.

Fix 2

Put the forecast on a discipline

What I found: a 21% average forecast error, swinging both ways. The same error produces excess stock in two regions and stock-outs in three, on the same products, in the same months.

What it costs: an estimated $340K a year in carrying cost on the excess, before counting the sales lost on the 11 fast movers that keep running out.

The fix: forecast discipline. One monthly forecast per distributor for the top products, the error published next to it every month, and a short review that asks only one question: why did we miss?

Fix 3

Measure every distributor on profit, then act on the tail

What I found: 101 distributors have never had a profit figure attached to them. On the sample data, the bottom 40 by shipments contribute about 2% of gross margin while taking roughly 18% of field support time.

What it costs: field time and support cost spent where the return is thinnest, while the distributors in the middle, the ones with room to grow, get what is left.

The fix: a distributor ROI view for all 132: revenue, incentive cost, freight, returns, and support time in one place. Segment the channel into grow, fix, and exit conversations, in that order. Fix before exit, always.
