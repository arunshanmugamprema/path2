Case Study · Bajaj Electricals

Distributor ROI Optimization

Aligning channel investment with partner performance. At Bajaj Electricals I ran distributor networks for 10+ years: a $100M P&L, 200+ distributors and 40,000+ retail accounts across 15 regions and 95 territories. The network was selling, but its economics had drifted. Distributor returns were thin, market credit was bloated, and churn kept eating the base.

The challenge

  • Distributor ROI stuck at 18 to 20 percent, too thin to keep good partners investing in the brand.
  • Market credit sitting at 1.5 to 2.0 times monthly sales, locking up working capital across the network.
  • Incentive and credit practices rewarding billing, not collection, so reported sales ran ahead of cash.
  • Distributor churn quietly shrinking the network year after year.

What changed

  • ROI measured distributor by distributor, not as a network average, so weak economics could not hide inside the total.
  • Credit norms reset against each distributor's actual monthly sales.
  • Incentive payout tied to collection rather than billing.
  • Underperforming distributors put on a structured improvement path before any exit decision.
